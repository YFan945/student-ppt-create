# Planning from source material

Use the user's material, audience, speaking time and rubric to decide scope and page order. A project defense may need design decisions and working evidence; a research talk may need methods and interpretation; a showcase may need actual work and process imagery. These are information needs, not fixed page sequences.

Preserve concrete explanations, cases, methods, observations and results. Group related material when that makes it easier to understand, and separate it when it deserves fuller treatment. Do not add a generic background, limitation, slogan or thank-you page merely to complete a template. If material is missing, identify it before designing the affected content.

For English talks, match vocabulary to the course and speaker, preserve the subject's meaning, and make notes speakable. For group talks, record ownership and transitions without prescribing a single page arrangement.
